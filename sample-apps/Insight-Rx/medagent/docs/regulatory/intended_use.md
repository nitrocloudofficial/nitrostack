## 2. Intended Use Statement

> The Autonomous Medical Imaging Diagnosis & Clinical Decision Agent is a software-only
> clinical decision support device intended to **assist qualified, licensed radiologists**
> in the review and interpretation of frontal chest radiographs in adult patients, by
> presenting a prioritized candidate finding, a localizing region of interest, an
> explainability heatmap, and references to retrieved clinical practice guidelines.
>
> The device is intended to be used **concurrently with, and subordinate to, the
> interpreting radiologist's own independent review of the original images.** All output
> is advisory. The device does not produce a diagnosis, does not produce a final report,
> and does not direct patient management.
>
> **Every output of the device requires review, and explicit affirmative acceptance, by a
> qualified radiologist before it may be entered into a patient record or used to inform
> any clinical decision.** The device is incapable of finalizing a report without that
> acceptance; this is enforced in software (see §4).
>
> The device is **not** intended to be used as a standalone or autonomous diagnostic
> device, is **not** intended to replace the radiologist's independent judgment, is
> **not** intended to be used as the sole basis for any diagnostic or treatment decision,
> and is **not** intended for use in the triage, prioritization, or exclusion of cases
> from human review.

### 2.1 Why this phrasing is drawn narrowly

The scope above is deliberately restrictive, and the restrictions are load-bearing rather
than cosmetic:

- **Assistive, concurrent-read positioning.** A device that outputs a diagnosis, or that
  removes cases from a radiologist's worklist, carries materially higher risk and a
  correspondingly heavier regulatory burden. Positioning the device as a concurrent
  second reader whose every output is affirmatively accepted by a clinician keeps the
  radiologist — not the software — as the decision-maker.
- **No triage or notification claim.** We explicitly disclaim worklist prioritization.
  Computer-assisted triage and notification carries its own classification and its own
  risk profile (a mis-prioritized case can delay care), and we have built nothing to
  support such a claim.
- **No autonomous claim.** An autonomous diagnostic claim on chest radiography would
  invite a substantially higher-risk classification. Nothing in this system is built for
  that, and the human-review gate in §4 makes it architecturally unavailable.

**A caution counsel should be aware of:** we understand that the Clinical Decision Support
carve-out from the device definition introduced by the 21st Century Cures Act
(FD&C Act §520(o)(1)(E)) **does not apply to software that acquires, processes, or
analyzes a medical image.** This system analyzes medical images. We therefore assume this
software **is a regulated device** and do not intend to argue otherwise. Restricting the
intended use is about reducing the *class* and risk profile, not about escaping device
status. Counsel to confirm.

## 3. Indications, users, and population

### 3.1 Indications for use
Adjunctive analysis of frontal (PA or AP) chest radiographs in adults, to support a
radiologist's evaluation for pulmonary opacity consistent with pneumonia and other
parenchymal abnormality.

### 3.2 Intended users
Qualified, licensed radiologists (or physicians with equivalent credentialed privileges
to interpret chest radiographs) practising within their scope. The device assumes a user
who is fully capable of interpreting the study **without** it and who is professionally
responsible for the interpretation. It is not intended for use by non-radiologist
clinicians, technologists, trainees acting without supervision, or patients.

### 3.3 Intended patient population
Adult patients (≥18 years) undergoing frontal chest radiography as part of routine care.

### 3.4 Intended use environment
Hospital and outpatient imaging settings, integrated with existing PACS/RIS workflow.
Deployed on-premise; capable of running fully offline.

## 4. The human-in-the-loop guarantee

The assistive positioning in §2 is not a labelling claim we ask reviewers to take on
trust. It is a structural property of the software, enforced at two points and testable:

1. **A mandatory review interrupt.** The orchestration graph
   (`src/medagent/agents/orchestrator.py`) suspends at a `human_review` node and cannot
   proceed past it without an explicit, authenticated clinician decision. No execution
   path reaches report finalization without passing through it.
2. **Role-based authorization of that decision.** Approving or revising a report requires
   a user holding the `radiologist` role (`src/medagent/security/auth.py`). An
   administrative user cannot approve or revise a clinical report — administering the
   system does not confer authority to sign an interpretation. Every decision, and every
   *refused* attempt, is recorded in a tamper-evident audit trail.

Consequences that follow structurally, not by policy:
- The device cannot emit a finalized report autonomously.
- The device cannot remove a case from human review.
- A case whose PHI de-identification fails is **halted**, not degraded and passed on.
- A case whose vision-model confidence falls below threshold is escalated to a human
  rather than being resolved automatically.

## 5. Limitations, contraindications, and out-of-scope use

The device must not be used for, and has not been designed or evaluated for:

| Out of scope | Note |
|---|---|
| Lateral or non-frontal projections | Only frontal (PA/AP) is in scope |
| Paediatric patients (<18 years) | No paediatric validation |
| Non-radiographic modalities (CT, MRI, US) | Chest radiography only |
| Autonomous or unsupervised interpretation | Prohibited; see §2 |
| Worklist triage or prioritization | Explicitly disclaimed; see §2.1 |
| Emergency or time-critical notification | No triage/notification claim |
| Sole basis for treatment decisions | Adjunct only |
| Any subgroup flagged in the Model Card | See below |

**Subgroup restriction.** The Model Card's subgroup analysis flags demographic strata that
are underrepresented or that violate a sensitivity/fairness threshold. The device does not
support a clinical claim for any flagged subgroup pending additional validation data.

**Known technical limitations:**
- Guideline retrieval is limited to the specific corpus indexed at deployment; it is not a
  comprehensive literature service, and retrieved text is not a treatment recommendation.
- The report-drafting and reasoning components use a large language model. LLM output is
  constrained by deterministic verification (schema conformance, agreement with the vision
  layer, and citation grounding — `src/medagent/agents/verification_checks.py`), but these
  are bounded checks, not a guarantee of clinical correctness. **This is a principal reason
  the human-review gate is mandatory and non-bypassable.**
- Performance on images from scanner models, acquisition protocols, or populations not
  represented in the (future) validation set is unknown.

## 6. Residual risk accepted by this intended use

| Risk | Mitigation | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| False negative (missed finding) | Operating threshold selected for high sensitivity; radiologist performs independent review regardless | Radiologist retains full diagnostic responsibility |
| False positive | Advisory output only; radiologist adjudicates | Possible additional review time |
| Automation bias (over-reliance) | Assistive-only labelling; explainability heatmap enables verification against the image; low-confidence cases escalate rather than assert | **Not eliminated.** Requires labelling, training, and human-factors validation (not yet performed — see gap analysis in REG-002) |
| Fabricated guideline citation | Deterministic citation-grounding check rejects any citation not present in retrieved evidence | Bounded to the retrieved corpus |
| PHI disclosure | De-identification hard gate: DICOM tag scrubbing, burned-in-pixel OCR redaction, NLP-based text de-identification; failure halts the case | Documented in REG-002 |
