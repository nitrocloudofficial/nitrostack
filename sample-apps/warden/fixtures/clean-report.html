Ransomware Campaign Targets Indian Financial Institutions

ThreatWire Security Research · Published June 2026

Over the past three weeks, ThreatWire researchers have tracked a coordinated ransomware campaign targeting mid-size banks and financial services firms across India. The intrusion set overlaps significantly with infrastructure and tooling previously attributed to Lazarus, and shares tactical similarities with known LockBit affiliate playbooks.

Initial access in observed incidents was consistent with T1190 (Exploit Public-Facing Application), with attackers targeting internet-facing portals running outdated software. Two vulnerabilities were observed being actively weaponised in this campaign: CVE-2023-34362, the Progress MOVEit Transfer SQL injection vulnerability, and CVE-2024-3400, a command injection flaw in Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS. Both have public proof-of-concept exploit code and have seen exploitation in the wild well beyond this campaign.

Post-compromise activity included lateral movement via compromised domain administrator credentials and staging of encryption payloads consistent with double-extortion ransomware operations. Command and control traffic was observed communicating with infrastructure at IP address 185.220.101.47 and the domain secure-updates-portal.xyz, both of which have since been flagged by multiple threat feeds.

The financial services sector accounted for roughly 40% of observed victims in this campaign, with the remainder spread across insurance and retail banking subsidiaries.

Defenders are advised to prioritise patching of both CVEs referenced above, review public-facing applications for exposure consistent with T1190, and monitor for outbound connections to the indicators listed. Organisations that have not yet applied vendor patches for either vulnerability should treat this as an active, ongoing threat rather than a theoretical risk.
